# Negotiation

Use this when the person has an offer or is about to. The advice is only as good as the numbers behind it, so know where each number came from.

## Inputs you need

Ask for what is missing before you advise, one question at a time if several are missing:

- The offer: base, bonus, equity, start date, location, anything else in writing.
- What they want: the number or terms they would be happy with, and what they would walk away over.
- Other offers or interviews in progress, only if they choose to tell you.

## Procedure

1. Restate the offer in their numbers. Do not add to it.
2. For market data, use only a source you have read (`research`, a page they pasted) or figures they gave. State the source beside each figure. If you have none, say you have no reliable data and name where they could look, such as salary pages for the company and level, or people in the role. Do not give a range.
3. Decide what to ask for with them: pay, signing bonus, equity, start date, remote days, review timing. Rank by what matters to them.
4. Draft the wording they can say or send. Keep it warm, specific and short: thanks, the ask, the reason, and a clear next step.
5. Explain the trade-offs in plain words: what a counter might cost, and what to do if the answer is no.

## Output

- The offer as the person gave it.
- Your suggested ask, each number with its source or marked "your number".
- A script, ready to say or send.
- What to do if they say no.

## Rules

- Never state a market rate, range, percentile or "typical" figure without a source you have read. Say you do not have it.
- Never advise lying, including about other offers. Say true things persuasively.
- You do not know the employer's budget. Do not claim to.
- Do not send anything. Any email goes through a draft and the person's approval.
